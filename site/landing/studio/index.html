01 The Writer, at work

From blank page to booth.

One dragon, one knight, one very bad night’s sleep. Scroll, and watch a scene get written, marked, organised and voiced in the VoxDown Writer.

    1. A blank page. A sleepy dragon.

      Start the way you always do: type. It’s plain text, so there’s nothing to learn before the first sentence.

    2. Who said that?

      Put the cursor in a quote and press Ctrl+K. Gerald gets his name, his colour and a card in the Cast, and every word he says is underlined in it.

    3. And how did he say it?

      A direction in brackets tells the narrator, or the voice, exactly how a line should land: deep, polite and utterly unbothered.

    4. What do we hear?

      Sounds of the world go in square brackets, the dragon’s own snore goes after his name, and a beat of silence goes in braces.

    5. Then the knight gets brave.

      A second scene, and the Scenes list and the board step in: chapters as columns, scenes as cards. Drag a card and its text moves with it.

    6. A note nobody else will read.

      Leave yourself a comment right in the text. It’s never read aloud and never printed, and Word gets it as a real comment.

    7. Not sure about “smite”?

      Cut the line to Scraps. It waits beside the story, in a file of its own, until you want it back.

    8. The book.

      Open the preview and the marks step aside: the same words, set on a page for print, Word or EPUB.

    9. The script.

      Every line with its speaker and its direction. That’s a table read, ready to go.

    10. Gerald’s sides.

      Each performer gets only their own lines, with the cue before each one. Hand them over, and the booth knows what to do.

    11. The audiobook.

      Make an audiobook… gives each character their own voice through ElevenLabs, OpenAI or Azure on your account, or Kokoro free on your own computer. This scene was voiced from this very file.

      03 Production-ready

      Straight to the booth. Or straight to the engine.

      The file already knows who says every line and how, so nobody has to work it out again.

      To people

      A narrator or a cast of actors

      Hand over a clear script with every speaker and direction, each performer’s sides, and your notes to the performer, which are never read aloud.

      • The script
      • Sides, per performer
      • Lines as CSV or JSON
      • A production file for audio tools

      To an engine

      An audiobook, a voice per character

      Make an audiobook… casts each character and voices every line, through a small connector you run on your computer.

      04 The real thing

      No mock-ups from here on.

      These are screens from the Writer itself, with the dragon story open.

      • The scene board

        Chapters as columns, scenes as cards. Drag one, or press Alt+↑/↓, and the text follows.

      • Bring your own AI, if you like

        Copy a request into any AI chat, paste the answer back, check every suggestion. Nothing is sent until you press the button.

      • Six looks

        Auto, Paper, Sepia, Night, Slate and High contrast, with your own font, size and spacing.

      Free, open and yours

      • Private by design

        Your story, your computer.

        Nothing is sent to us; there’s no VoxDown server to send it to. Files open from your computer and save back there: in place in Chrome and Edge, as a downloaded copy elsewhere. No account, no analytics, no ads.

      • $0

        No subscription and no sign-up. Audiobooks can be free too, with Kokoro on your own computer.

      • MIT

        The Writer and every tool are open source, and the spec is CC BY 4.0. Once loaded, the Writer works offline.
