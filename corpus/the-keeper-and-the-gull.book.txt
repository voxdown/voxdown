# The Keeper and the Gull

A story in three short parts.

## I. The storm

Maud had kept the light at Carrick Point for thirty-one years, and in all that time she'd never once been late with the lamp. Tonight the stairs felt longer than usual.

"One hundred and twelve," she counted at the top. "Same as yesterday. Same as always."

A gull sat on the rail outside, feathers flat against the gale.

"Oh, it's you again." She opened the hatch an inch. "In or out. I'm not heating the whole of Cornwall."

The gull came in, shook itself, and looked at her as if _she_ were the visitor.

“I prefer ‘guest’.”

Maud sat down rather suddenly on the paraffin can.

***

## II. The song

“There was a ship called the _Mary Anne_,
And she sailed from Fowey to Rye;
She carried salt and she carried tin,
And she never came home to dry.”

“That’s not how it goes.”

“It’s how _I_ sing it. Besides, nobody remembers the real one.”

They say the keeper before you heard the same song.
They say she sang it back.

“Who taught you that?”
“Nobody teaches a gull anything.”

“Well, I won’t sing it.” She trimmed the wick.

“Maud! Maud, are you up there? There’s a light out past the Manacles, and it’s no light of ours.

“I’ve rowed out twice and it moves when I move. I don’t like it, Maud, and you know I don’t scare easy.

“Will you come down?”

'Give me a minute, Tom!' she shouted. 'And don't you dare row out a third time.'

***

## III. The light

The three of them stood on the gallery: the keeper, the boatman, and the gull, who'd insisted. Tom's lantern swung from a 6" hook by the door.

"There. See it?"

“That’s no ship. That’s the _Mary Anne_.” “She’s come to dry at last.”

Out on the water, the strange light dipped once, as if it bowed, and went out.

“Well,” she said, after a long while. “Same as always.” “Kettle’s on, if anyone wants it.”

“Is there toast?”
