I

Chapter I · The marks

Mark it the way an editor would.

Anyone reading a story aloud asks the page four questions: who is speaking, how, what do we hear, and where do we wait. VoxDown answers each with a mark small enough to leave your prose alone.

  1. 1

    Who is speaking?

    Write the speaker’s name before the line: @King, @Percival. A line with no name belongs to the narrator, so plain prose stays plain.

  2. 2

    How is it said?

    A direction follows the name in brackets: (grandly, to a crowded hall). The narrator takes one too, with @(warmly).

  3. 3

    What do we hear?

    Square brackets are sounds in the world: [armor squeaking]. Stars stress a word and set it in italics in the book. When the moment needs a breath, {pause}.

  4. 4

    Then the pencil sets.

    What was in the margin is now in the line, and it still reads like a story. It is plain text: any editor opens it, Git diffs it, and nothing is locked inside an app. This is VoxDown.

The manuscript, as typedMarked in red pencilSet in VoxDown

Chapter III · The desk

    
A writing desk that knows who is speaking.

    
The VoxDown Writer is a calm, serious place to write fiction, scripts and audio drama, with the whole book within reach. It is free, and it opens in your browser.

  


  

    
    
    
      
  1. 1
    The workbench. Scenes, cast, scraps, comments and warnings, a tab each, with an outline to jump anywhere.
    2. 
      
  2. 2
    Who and How. Give a passage its speaker with Ctrl+K and its direction with Ctrl+J; “Next quote with no speaker” finds the next one.
    3. 
      
  3. 3
    Marks that keep out of the way. Each speaker gets a colour. Show marks as written, or drawn: names as tags, directions as small notes above the words.
    4. 
      
  4. 4
    Hear. F9 reads from the cursor in your computer’s own voices, one per character where it has them.

  5. 5

Appendix · A key to the marks

    
Plain text underneath.

    
A .vxd file is Markdown with a few marks. Any editor opens it, Git diffs it, and a Markdown viewer still shows a readable story.

  

  

    

      
@Mara “Who’s there?”
Who says it. A line with no name is the narrator’s.

      
@Mara (softly)
How it is said.

      
@(grimly) The door…
A direction for the narrator; @() ends it early.

      
*never*
A stressed word, and italics in the book.

      
[door slams]
A sound in the world.

      
@Mara [laughs]
A speaker’s own sound; @[sighs] inside a quote.

      
{pause 2s} {page}
A silence; a page turn for a picture book.

      
::: @Wanda reading the letter
A run of lines under one voice or direction.

      
{book only} {audio only}
What the book prints and what the audio hears.

      
%% louder each line %%
A note for the performer, never read aloud.

      
<!-- check this -->
Your own comment, never read or printed.

Chapter V · Colophon

  
Your story stays on your computer.

  
There’s no VoxDown server to send it to. The Writer runs in your browser, opens your files and saves them back where they were. No account, no analytics, no ads. AI and online voices are used only if you choose them, on your own account.

  
VoxDown is free and open source under the MIT license; its specification is CC BY 4.0. Nothing is held back for a paid tier, because there isn’t one.
