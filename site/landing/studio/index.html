01 The Writer, at work

From blank page to booth.

One dragon, one knight, one very bad night’s sleep. Scroll, and watch a scene get written, marked, organised and voiced in the VoxDown Writer.

    1. A blank page. A sleepy dragon.

      Start the way you always do: type. It’s plain text, so there’s nothing to learn before the first sentence.

    2. Who said that? You tell it.

      Put the cursor in a quote and give it its speaker. The name goes before the quote, the line takes their colour, and they get a card in the Cast.

    3. Deep, polite and utterly unbothered.

      Directions in round brackets say how a line lands. Square brackets hold what the world sounds like, and braces hold a beat of silence.

    4. Then the knight gets brave.

      A second scene, and the scene board opens beside the page: each scene a card, with a line and a state. Move a card and its text moves with it.

    5. Notes to self. Lines on ice.

      Leave yourself a comment that’s never read aloud or printed. Not sure about “smite”? Cut it to Scraps, where it waits in a file beside the story.

    6. Three handouts, zero retyping.

      The script for the table read, the book for readers, and each character’s lines for the booth, all from the same file.

    7. Press play. That’s the file talking.

      Make an audiobook… gives each character a voice: ElevenLabs, OpenAI or Azure on your own account, or Kokoro, free on your computer. This scene was voiced from this very file.

      05 For developers

      Open all the way down.

      A published spec, a parser, renderers, an editor component and a command line. Build your own reader, your own voices, your own tools.

      The specCheat sheetPlaygroundGitHub

      $ npm install voxdown
$ npx @voxdown/cli check story.vxd
      The packages
      • voxdownParse a story: its cast, lines and marks.
      • @voxdown/renderThe book and the script, as HTML.
      • @voxdown/editorAn editor as a web component.
      • @voxdown/clivxd check · convert · connect · tag · produce · mcp
      • VS CodeColours, warnings and a preview.

      Free, open and yours

      • Private by design

        Your story, your computer.

        Nothing is sent to us; there’s no VoxDown server to send it to. Files open from your computer and save back there: in place in Chrome and Edge, as a downloaded copy elsewhere.

        • No account
        • No analytics
        • No ads
        • AI and voices only if you choose them

      • $0

        No subscription and no sign-up. Audiobooks can be free too, with Kokoro on your own computer.

      • MIT

        The Writer and every tool are open source, and the spec is CC BY 4.0. Once loaded, the Writer works offline.
